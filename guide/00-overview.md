# Teaching AI to Ask: The VERIFY Framework

*Teacher Guide · Sandra Robinson · Big Wella*

## What this guide is

A practical framework for teaching secondary students to think with AI rather than offload thinking to it. VERIFY names six steps — **V**alidate, **E**xamine, **R**eflect, **I**nvestigate, **F**ilter, **Y**our judgement — and gives teachers the moves to make each step happen in their classroom.

The framework is grounded in cognitive science (cognitive offloading, productive struggle, metacognitive laziness) and in Australian classroom equity research. It is not a checklist for students to run independently. It is a teaching framework that requires a teacher who is present.

## How to use this guide

You do not need to implement every section at once. Start with one letter. Use it consistently. Add the next when the first feels embedded.

| Part | What it covers |
|------|---------------|
| [Part 1 — The Problem With Easy Answers](01-the-problem.md) | Why the framework exists. What cognitive offloading costs students. What VERIFY asks of you as a teacher. |
| [Part 2 — The Framework](02-the-framework.md) | Each letter in depth. Mechanisms, classroom realities, practitioner moves, the seven F check questions, the Y diagnostic. |
| [Part 3 — Using VERIFY in Practice](03-in-practice.md) | Before / during / after teacher moves for any task. Four worked examples (English, Science, History, Technology). |
| [Part 4 — Assessment and Accountability](04-assessment.md) | Task design principles. Two full rubrics (A–E and four-level). Feedback language for every letter. |
| [Appendix — Research Foundation](appendix-research.md) | The research base. Plain-language summaries of cognitive offloading, productive struggle, Meta-Task Awareness, the illusion of mastery, equity in Australian AI access, and the Chain of Learning. Full source list. |

## How to read it

- **Reading the framework whole:** Parts 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → Appendix. The first read takes about an hour.
- **Introducing one letter to a class:** Read Part 1 first. Then go to the section in Part 2 for the letter you are introducing. Use the Part 3 teacher moves for that letter.
- **Designing assessment:** Part 4. Use whichever rubric format fits your reporting context.
- **Justifying the approach to a colleague or leader:** Part 1 and the Appendix.
