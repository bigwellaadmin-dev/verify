# VERIFY Rubric (A-E)

*Aligned to QCAA standards elaboration format. C represents the achievement standard.*

This rubric assesses the VERIFY process, not the quality of the final product. It is designed to be used alongside subject-specific rubrics that assess content knowledge and skill, not instead of them.

**Source:** Sandra Robinson, *Teaching AI to Ask: The VERIFY Framework, Part 4: Assessment and Accountability*. Licensed CC BY 4.0.

| Criterion | A — Extending | B — Above standard | C — Achievement standard | D — Approaching standard | E — Below standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **V** — Validate the Task | Constructs a precise, multi-layered prompt specification that fully defines task, audience, thinking type, and constraints. Demonstrates sophisticated understanding of how prompt framing shapes output quality. | Constructs a clear prompt specification addressing task, audience, and thinking type. Makes deliberate choices about framing and can explain the reasoning behind them. | Constructs a prompt specification that addresses the task and audience. Identifies the kind of thinking required before opening the AI. | Attempts a prompt specification but it is incomplete or vague. May have copied the task without reframing it. Limited evidence of pre-AI thinking. | No prompt specification evident. Copies task directly into AI without modification. No evidence of task validation before prompting. |
| **E** — Examine the Source | Systematically interrogates AI output for accuracy, perspective, and gap. Independently traces claims to primary sources. Identifies and names structural biases in the output. | Checks accuracy of key claims against identified sources. Recognises and names at least one perspective gap or bias. Demonstrates understanding of AI as a partial source. | Identifies sources for the most important claims. Recognises that AI output may be incomplete or biased. Attempts to verify at least one claim independently. | Accepts most AI output without interrogation. Makes a surface-level attempt to check one claim. Limited understanding of AI as a source with perspective. | Accepts AI output without any examination. No attempt to verify claims or identify perspective. Treats AI output as authoritative. |
| **R** — Reflect on Needs and Context | Demonstrates rigorous metacognitive awareness. Clearly articulates how the output does and does not fit the specific task, and makes targeted revisions based on this analysis. | Reflects thoughtfully on fit between output and task context. Identifies specific gaps and addresses most of them. Can articulate the difference between what the AI provided and what the task required. | Reflects on whether the output fits the task and their context. Identifies at least one gap or mismatch and attempts to address it. Shows evidence of asking whether they have thought about the task. | Reflection is surface level or generic. May acknowledge that the output could be improved without identifying specific gaps. Limited evidence of metacognitive awareness. | No evidence of reflection. Accepts AI output as complete without asking whether it fits the task or their context. |
| **I** — Investigate Further | Identifies specific gaps in AI output and pursues targeted investigation from multiple independent sources. Incorporates findings that substantively change or deepen the work. | Identifies at least one significant gap and investigates it from an independent source. Findings are integrated meaningfully into the work. | Identifies something the AI output does not provide for their specific context and makes an attempt to find it. Investigation is relevant to the task. | Makes a superficial attempt to investigate further. May add one additional source without explaining why it was chosen or how it addresses a gap. | No investigation beyond AI output. Accepts what was provided as sufficient without asking what might be missing. |
| **F** — Filter for Quality and Fairness | Applies the full set of check questions with precision. Makes multiple targeted changes to the work as a result. Can articulate specifically what was changed and why, including fairness considerations. | Applies most check questions and makes at least two substantive changes based on them. Identifies and addresses at least one fairness gap. | Applies at least two check questions and makes at least one substantive change to the work as a result. Identifies a quality or fairness issue and responds to it. | Applies check questions superficially. May make one minor change or none. Limited ability to identify quality or fairness issues in AI output. | No evidence of filtering. Does not apply check questions. Cannot identify quality or fairness issues in the submitted work. |
| **Y** — Your Own Judgement | Demonstrates full intellectual ownership. Defends reasoning fluently and specifically, including under challenge. Work is recognisably the student's own thinking, shaped by but not dependent on AI output. | Demonstrates clear ownership of the work's argument and conclusions. Can explain the reasoning behind key decisions and respond to at least one challenge question. | Can explain what they decided and why, in their own words, without referring to the work. Demonstrates that the judgement in the work is their own. | Can summarise the work but struggles to explain the reasoning behind specific decisions. Ownership of the argument is unclear. | Cannot explain the reasoning in the work independently of the text. The judgement in the work does not appear to be the student's own. |
